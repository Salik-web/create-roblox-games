# Narrative, Writing & Tone

## Story in Roblox games
Players skim. Story is told through **places, objects and events**, with tiny bits of
text. Aim: understandable with zero reading, richer for those who read.

## Story kit (fill in GDD)
- Premise (1 sentence), the player's role, the antagonist/obstacle and their motive.
- 3–5 beats: inciting moment (first 30 s) → escalation → twist → climax → ending(s).
- Environmental storytelling: notes, posters, audio logs, graffiti, objects arranged
  to imply events. Collectible lore pages = retention + completionist goal.
- Multiple endings / secret ending for replay and social media theories.
- Named, memorable characters with a visual gimmick and catchphrase (original).

## Writing style
- UI text: 2–6 words. Dialogue: ≤ 2 short lines per bubble. Typewriter effect with
  skip. Use humour even in horror (relief = pacing).
- Readable for young & non-native English players; localise later
  (`localization-accessibility.md`).
- Names: short, punchy, searchable. Game title: 1–4 words, clear genre signal.

## Meme humour (original)
- Absurd contrast (terrifying monster that's actually a giant rubber duck with a
  tie), anti-climax, deadpan signage, overreaction effects, silly death messages,
  ridiculous items with serious descriptions.
- Never copy existing meme characters, catchphrases, songs or creators.
- Keep it inoffensive: no slurs, no real people, no politics/religion jokes, no
  shock content — required for maturity labels and broad appeal.

## Horror pacing
Calm → unease → threat → peak → release. Foreshadow with sound and light. Rule of
three for scares (two fakes, one real). Let players laugh after a scare.

## Delivery tools
Dialogue system (`npc-ai.md`), cutscene camera, subtitles for voice/whispers, notes UI,
signage decals (generate or design), chapter title cards, end screen with stats and
"tell your friends" moment.
